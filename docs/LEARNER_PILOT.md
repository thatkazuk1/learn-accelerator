# First Learner Pilot

Run a 15-20 minute session with 3-5 IT students or learners with comparable
experience. Ask permission before taking notes. Use participant numbers,
not names, and do not request personal or academic information.

## Before Play

Ask what a router, switch, and access point each do. Ask whether a phone can
be connected to Wi-Fi without being able to reach the internet. Record the
reasoning in the learner's own words without correcting it yet.

## During Play

Ask the learner to bring the desktop and phone online. Avoid demonstrating
the intended topology. If they become stuck, first ask what they expect a
device or connection to do. Hints are allowed and are not a failure.

Observe:

- Can they discover placement, cabling, and wireless association?
- Do they distinguish a physical link from internet connectivity?
- Do diagnostic messages help them choose their next action?
- Can they make and explain a prediction before testing a disconnection?
- Can they complete the independent layout with fewer prompts?

Note confusing controls separately from networking misconceptions.

## After Play

Read the learner's saved explanations together. Ask why the switch was useful
with this router and why Wi-Fi can remain connected after removing the AP
uplink. Compare their reasoning with the before-play answers.

Use a simple note table:

| Participant | Before-play reasoning | Sticking points | Hints used | Independent completion | After-play reasoning |
| ----------- | --------------------- | --------------- | ---------- | ---------------------- | -------------------- |
| P01         |                       |                 |            |                        |                      |

Prioritize issues repeated across participants. Treat the findings as early
design feedback, not proof of broad learning effectiveness. Do not use the
game's explanation-length check as a measure of understanding.
